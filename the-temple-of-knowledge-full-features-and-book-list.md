## CALCism creator-curated information

CALCism — Cosmic Anarcho-Libertarian Communalism
Creator/reference name: Sora Izumi Dao (空 泉 道)
Motto: “No theory is sacred enough to override reality.”

Philosophical foundations: materialism/physicalism and methodological naturalism; critical inquiry and evidence; selected influences from Buddhism (impermanence and compassion), Daoism (wu wei) and Confucianism (responsibility and relational care). These are selective influences, not claims that traditions are identical.

Political and economic orientation: anarchist communism, communalism, worker self-management, common/social ownership, mutual aid, free association, direct democracy and confederation. Proposed transition: Platformism → Social Mutualism → Social Collectivism → a stateless councilist confederacy.

Ethics and method: moral anti-realism/error-theory-informed analysis, material consequentialism and practical virtue; examine causes, evidence, effects and flourishing rather than treating “good” and “evil” as objective properties. Competing theories and objections remain open to consideration.

Wider horizon: ecological responsibility, ecocentrism, cosmopolitan solidarity, anti-authoritarianism, feminism and cultural-philosophical syncretism. CALCism is a revisable synthesis, not an established academic consensus or unquestionable doctrine.

## Moral philosophy pathway

Moral Anti-Realism, Moral Nihilism & the Illusion of Good and Evil
Moral error theory: ordinary moral claims purport to describe objective moral facts, but such facts do not exist. Moral nihilism rejects morality in a specified sense, often objective or categorical moral truths. Moral anti-realism is a broader family of views denying mind-independent moral facts. Moral subjectivism grounds judgements in attitudes or perspectives. Moral relativism makes judgements dependent on cultural or conceptual frameworks. Moral naturalism is a contrasting realist family that seeks to ground moral facts in natural facts. These positions are not synonyms. Nietzsche critiques inherited moral systems but should not simply be classified as a modern error theorist. Sam Harris's The Moral Landscape is included as a contrasting argument against moral scepticism.
